## 2. The risks of badminton
Badminton involves sudden starts, stops, lunges, jumps and changes of direction, fast-moving shuttlecocks and swinging racquets, often with other players close by. Taking part carries a risk of injury, some of it serious or permanent, including:
• sprains, strains, and muscle, tendon or ligament tears (such as an Achilles tendon rupture), fractures, dislocations and joint injuries;
• eye and face injuries from a shuttlecock or racquet, which can cause permanent loss of sight;
• being hit by a racquet, a shuttlecock or another player, and collisions on the same or a neighbouring court;
• slips, trips and falls, including on the floor, court lines, moisture, bags or equipment;
• overexertion, dehydration, heat illness, and heart attack or other medical events brought on by exercise;
• harm arising from the conduct of other participants or from the condition of the facility or equipment.
These are examples, not a complete list. Some risks cannot be removed without changing the nature of the game.
