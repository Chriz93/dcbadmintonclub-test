# 30 — Waiver review for Ontario (September 12, 2026)

**This is not legal advice.** It is a plain-language review of the league's waiver against published commentary on
Ontario law, so that an Ontario lawyer can review the points below quickly. Nothing here — and nothing in the waiver —
claims that the organizer, volunteers or the school board are immune from liability. Whether any release is enforced is
decided by a court on the facts of a case.

## What was reviewed

- **Version 2026-09-v1**: the wording on the 2026–27 sign-up form, as committed in `aebbc28` and live on the league site
  (`legacy/waiver/2026-09-v1.txt`, extracted word for word).
- **Version 2026-09-v2**: a revised draft written for this review (`legacy/waiver/2026-09-v2.txt`). It is current on the

## Sources consulted

Secondary sources (law-firm commentary) were read. The primary statutes on e-Laws and CanLII could not be retrieved by the
tools used for this review, so every statutory reference below must be confirmed against the current consolidated text.

- Gowling WLG, *Sports and recreation: risk mitigation and occupiers' liability* (2019):
  https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2019/sports-and-recreation-risk-mitigation
  - It discusses the requirement in the *Occupiers' Liability Act* (OLA) s. 5(3) that an occupier take reasonable steps to
    bring a release to a signer's attention.
  - It describes *Hosseinkhani v. QK Fitness*: a gym's waiver was not relied on because nothing drew attention to it (for
    example, a "Please read carefully" or "Warning" heading, bold or red type, or a frame).
- Siskinds, *Liability waivers: are they enforceable?*: https://www.siskinds.com/liability-waivers-are-they-enforceable/
  - Ontario courts largely uphold waivers after *Schnarr v. Blue Mountain Resorts*, 2018 ONCA 313.
  - A waiver covers only what falls within its scope, and its signer must be given reasonable notice.
  - It is unenforceable if signed by a minor or by someone without capacity.
- McMillan LLP, *Limitations of Liability, Waivers, and Occupier Liability in Ontario* (search summary only; the page
  refused automated access): https://mcmillan.ca/insights/publications/limitations-of-liability-waivers-and-occupier-liability-in-ontario/
- Lerners LLP / Mondaq, *Court of Appeal holds that Waivers are Enforceable* (*Schnarr*):
  https://www.mondaq.com/canada/consumer-law/691802/court-of-appeal-holds-that-waivers-are-enforceable
- On minors: Mondaq, *Minors – The Special Case of Liability Waivers*
  (https://www.mondaq.com/canada/insurance-laws-and-products/833398/minors-the-special-case-of-liability-waivers) and
  Merovitz Potechin, *Parental Waivers for Children's Activities*
  (https://www.merovitzpotechin.com/parental-waivers-for-childrens-activities-are-they-valid/). These sources say that a
  release signed by a parent is generally not enforceable against the child, although Ontario has no definitive appellate
  ruling.
- On the *Consumer Protection Act, 2023* (Bill 142): Norton Rose Fulbright, BLG (January 2025) and Osler summaries.
  - The act received Royal Assent on December 6, 2023 and comes into force on a date to be proclaimed; the summaries read
    describe it as not yet in force.
  - Once in force, it lists prohibited terms in consumer contracts.
  - **Its current status in September 2026 was not confirmed and must be checked.**

## Points for an Ontario lawyer

1. **Occupier status.**
   - Is the organizer, as permit holder, an "occupier" of the gym under OLA s. 1? If so, is the form's presentation (the
     notice at the top, the scrollable text, a required tick box and typed name, and the record) enough for s. 5(3)?
   - Does OLA s. 4(1) (risks willingly assumed) matter here?
2. **Consumer protection.**
   - Is the organizer a "supplier" of services under the *Consumer Protection Act, 2002*? If so, how do ss. 7 and 9
     (deemed warranty of services, no waiver of rights) apply to a non-occupier? *Schnarr* resolved the conflict for
     occupiers.
   - Confirm the status of the *Consumer Protection Act, 2023* and whether any clause would be a prohibited term.
3. **Participants under 18.**
   - Enforceability against a minor; whether a parental indemnity is advisable or enforceable.
   - Whether the league should be adults only, or use a separate informed-consent and acknowledgement-of-risk form for
     minors.
4. **OCDSB.**
   - Check the facility permit's own waiver, indemnity and insurance requirements.
   - Should OCDSB be named as a Releasee in the organizer's document, and does the permit require the organizer to hold
     third-party liability insurance?
5. **Insurance.** Whether the organizer should carry liability insurance, and whether the waiver should mention it.
6. **Retention.**
   - How long to keep acceptance records, given the *Limitations Act, 2002*: the basic two-year period from discovery,
     the ultimate 15-year period, and suspension while a claimant is a minor.
   - How long to keep the Past Players data.
7. **Privacy law.** Whether PIPEDA applies to this activity (fees are collected), and how to handle the optional medical
   information.
8. **Electronic signature.** Whether a ticked box, a typed name and the stored record (the exact wording and its
   SHA-256, time in UTC and league time, device time zone, and sign-in email) meet the *Electronic Commerce Act, 2000*
   and would be persuasive evidence.
9. **Indemnity.** v2 deliberately has no indemnity clause; the lawyer should say whether one is appropriate.
10. **Fees.** Whether section 9 (no refunds except the season rules) raises any consumer-protection issue.

## What the site now records (for the lawyer's awareness)

- **For each acceptance:**
  - the exact version and its wording fingerprint (SHA-256);
  - the sign-in email, the name on the registration, the typed signature, and the player record;
  - the time accepted, in UTC and as shown in Ottawa time;
  - the time zone and offset the participant's device reported;
  - how it was accepted: the registration form, or a later acceptance of an updated version;
  - the registration reference;
  - adult or guardian, and the minor's name;
  - the separate photo/video choice (or that none was offered);
  - the device description.
- **Guarantees:**
  - Wording is never changed after it is published; the database refuses such a change. A change is a new version.
  - Records are never changed or deleted; if a player record is deleted, the record stays and the link is cleared.
  - Nothing is created for anyone who registered before records began. Consent is never back-filled.
- **Who can read them:** only the organizer, with the second sign-in factor, can read or download records. A player's
  page receives only that player's own records.
